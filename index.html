How it works.

Three things to tell it, about five minutes the first time. After that, a couple of minutes once a month.

  1. Add your balances

    Each account in its own currency: current account, savings, pension, brokerage, the car loan. What it says today is enough.

  2. Say what you earn and spend

    A typical month, roughly. Add a raise, a return on investments, or a bonus that lands in March if you want to.

  3. Read where it's heading

    Net worth for every month ahead, what you could reach, how long it would last if income stopped, and when it hits your target.

Once a month, open Update balances and type each new figure on one screen. History then tells you what changed and which account did it.

What one year is made of

Money in, money out, growth, interest, and each planned bonus or bill by name. Then each account, now against a year from now.

Typed one wrong? Take it back

Swipe a balance away and every figure goes back to what it said without it. Put it back any time.

What it doesn't do

No transactions to track, no bank connections, no receipts, no budgets. Balances and monthly totals in, and that is all it needs.

Growth

Savings earn nothing unless you say so.

Cash accumulates as cash. Returns apply only to accounts you mark as growing, and interest pays out as cash unless you keep it in the account.

History

Updates keep the old balance.

Every update is a new entry, never an edit, so you can see how your projections held up.

Exact

Counted to the cent.

Money is stored as whole cents, never as floating-point numbers, so nothing drifts a cent at a time.

Honesty

A falling line is drawn falling.

Spend more than you earn and the line goes down. Never clamped at zero, never smoothed.

Made for money in more than one country.

Questions.

The ones people ask first.

Do I have to update balances by hand?

A balance is a number you type, once a month is plenty, and the app reminds you if you ask it to. From version 1.1 you can also add the statements your banks send you: WaffleWealth reads them on your iPhone, checks every figure against the statement's own totals, and adds the balances and lines. From a computer, upload them at WaffleWealth on the web.

Why doesn't it connect to my bank?

Because then your bank access would live on someone's server. WaffleWealth has no server and no account, so there is nowhere for your data to go. It also works with any bank in any country, including the pensions and deposits that bank connections can't reach.

Is this financial advice?

No. It reports what your own numbers add up to. The income, spending and returns you type are assumptions, not predictions, and nothing in the app tells you to save, spend, borrow or invest.

What happens to my data?

It stays in a database on your iPhone, encrypted while the phone is locked. From version 1.1, if you switch on iCloud in Settings, a copy goes into the private part of your own iCloud so you can use it from a computer; only your Apple ID can read it, and we can't. The details are in the privacy policy.

Does it work outside the US?

Yes. Every account keeps its own currency, any ISO currency is available, and you choose the one you see your net worth in.

Will it cost money?

It is free to download, and everything you enter is free, with no limit on accounts or currencies. If a paid tier comes later, it will add extra readings and never hold back your own data.

Can I try it before downloading?

Yes. The demo runs the same arithmetic in your browser, and the web page has an example to look around. Nothing you type there leaves the page.

WaffleWealth reports figures and does not advise. The numbers you give it are assumptions, not predictions, and nothing in the app is a recommendation to save, spend, borrow or invest. The projection above uses made-up example numbers.
